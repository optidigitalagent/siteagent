# Website Quality Bar

## A website must answer quickly

Within the first meaningful desktop and mobile viewport, a visitor should
understand:

- what the business offers;
- who it is for or in what situation it is relevant;
- why it is worth attention;
- what action to take.

Abstract atmosphere does not replace product identification.

## Scope must match evidence

### Full commercial site

Allowed only when evidence supports a concrete product/service plus enough
distinct offer, proof, media, or process themes to create meaningful sections.

### Intentional micro-site

Use for sparse but usable evidence. Keep it compact: one clear offer, one strong
visual idea, one concise supporting narrative, and one primary CTA. Do not pad it
into a long page.

### Insufficient evidence

If the actual product cannot be identified or the site would require invention,
block generation. Do not hide uncertainty behind words such as `experience`,
`premium`, `unique`, or `unforgettable`.

## Creative quality

- Start from business evidence, real media, audience, atmosphere, and goal.
- Generate materially different concepts, not palette variants.
- Select concepts from rendered desktop/mobile evidence.
- Preserve the selected central idea during full build.
- Use a business-derived signature element.
- Avoid universal narrow columns, anonymous rounded-card grids, decorative
  gradients, repeated dark CTA footers, and generic luxury/editorial systems.
- A beautiful art exercise that does not sell or explain the business is rejected.

## Content quality

Every section must add new information, desire, proof, decision support, or a
conversion step. A section is filler if it exists only for visual rhythm.

Missing prices, timing, availability, and other current details may be disclosed
once. They must not dominate the narrative or be repeated across sections.

Semantic repetition counts even when wording changes.

## Media quality

- Use real media intentionally.
- Match visual claims to media; do not promise `night` with unrelated daylight
  imagery unless the concept explains the relationship.
- Do not destroy useful imagery with excessive overlays.
- Respect resolution, aspect ratio, focal point, and mobile crop.
- Do not publish Instagram UI screenshots, duplicates, or fake proof.

## Review quality

Scores require evidence and deductions. A missing evaluation blocks acceptance.
Technical quality cannot compensate for weak business clarity, copy, story,
brand fit, or commercial usefulness.

The same agent that implemented the work must not be the only reviewer.
Screenshots and browser evidence are mandatory for visual approval.
